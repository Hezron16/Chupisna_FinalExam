Emergency Kit Guide

Essential go-bag supplies for every disaster.

A go-bag is a small bag you can grab in seconds. A home kit holds a larger supply to last at least three days. You do not have to buy everything at once: start with water, food and light, then add a little each week.

What to pack

Water and food

1 gallon (about 4 litres) per person per day, plus a 3-day supply of non-perishable food.

First aid

Bandages, antiseptic, gloves, masks and your regular medicines.

Radio and power

Battery or crank radio, flashlight, spare batteries and a power bank.

Documents

IDs, insurance, titles and medical records in a waterproof bag.

Hygiene and sanitation

Soap, sanitizer, wipes, toothbrush, sanitary items and garbage bags.

Clothing and shelter

Sturdy shoes, a change of clothes, an emergency blanket and a poncho.

Tight budget?

Reuse clean plastic bottles for water, pack rice, canned fish and biscuits you already eat, and use a sturdy school bag. Ask your barangay about free preparedness kits and training.

How to Pack Your Go-Bag

What goes into a grab-and-go emergency bag. About 25 seconds, no sound; the steps appear as large on-screen text. Original animation made for SANDIG (see credits).

Read the transcript (text version)
  1. Water and food: about 4 litres of water per person per day and 3 days of non-perishable food.
  2. First aid and medicines: bandages, antiseptic, gloves, masks and a 7-day supply of prescription medicines.
  3. Light and radio: flashlight, spare batteries, a battery or crank radio and a power bank.
  4. Documents and cash: copies of IDs and records in a waterproof bag, plus some cash in small bills.
  5. Think of everyone: baby supplies, eyeglasses, mobility aids and pet food if you need them.

SANDIG

Sandig means “to rely on” in Filipino. SANDIG helps Filipino families, schools and communities prepare for typhoons, floods, earthquakes, fires and volcanic eruptions. It supports UN Sustainable Development Goal 11: Sustainable Cities and Communities.
