Videos

Six short, original animations show the key steps for each emergency. They have no sound, so each step appears as large on-screen text. Captions and a written transcript are provided under every video.

Drop, Cover and Hold On

What to do in the first seconds of an earthquake. About 25 seconds, no sound; the steps appear as large on-screen text. Original animation made for SANDIG (see credits).

Read the transcript (text version)
  1. Drop: get down on your hands and knees right away.
  2. Cover: get under a sturdy table or desk, or cover your head and neck with your arms.
  3. Hold on: hold the table leg and stay put until the shaking stops.
  4. Then move: walk calmly to an open area, away from buildings, posts and glass.
  5. Stay informed: expect aftershocks, check PHIVOLCS advisories and call 911 for emergencies.

Getting Ready Before a Typhoon

Five things to do when a typhoon is approaching. About 25 seconds, no sound; the steps appear as large on-screen text. Original animation made for SANDIG (see credits).

Read the transcript (text version)
  1. Watch the bulletins: follow PAGASA and your barangay announcements.
  2. Secure your home: close windows and doors and move loose items indoors.
  3. Check your go-bag: water, food, medicines, flashlight, radio, documents and a charged power bank.
  4. Know where to go: know the way to your evacuation centre and leave early when officials say so.
  5. Stay connected: charge phones, write down emergency numbers and share your plan with family.

Flood Safety: Turn Around, Stay Safe

How to stay out of danger before and during a flood. About 25 seconds, no sound; the steps appear as large on-screen text. Original animation made for SANDIG (see credits).

Read the transcript (text version)
  1. Know your risk: find out if your street floods and where higher ground is.
  2. Move valuables up: lift documents, electronics and medicines early.
  3. Stay out of floodwater: never walk or drive through it.
  4. Go to higher ground: move to your meeting point or evacuation centre before the water rises.
  5. Switch off power: if water enters your home, switch off the main power if it is safe to do so.

Fire Safety: Get Low and Go

How to prevent fires and escape safely. About 25 seconds, no sound; the steps appear as large on-screen text. Original animation made for SANDIG (see credits).

Read the transcript (text version)
  1. Prevent fires: never leave cooking unattended and install smoke alarms where you can.
  2. Get low: smoke rises, so crawl low under smoke toward the exit.
  3. Feel doors: touch a door with the back of your hand and use another way out if it is hot.
  4. Stay out: once you are out, never go back inside for belongings.
  5. Call for help: call 911 or the Bureau of Fire Protection from a safe place.

How to Pack Your Go-Bag

What goes into a grab-and-go emergency bag. About 25 seconds, no sound; the steps appear as large on-screen text. Original animation made for SANDIG (see credits).

Read the transcript (text version)
  1. Water and food: about 4 litres of water per person per day and 3 days of non-perishable food.
  2. First aid and medicines: bandages, antiseptic, gloves, masks and a 7-day supply of prescription medicines.
  3. Light and radio: flashlight, spare batteries, a battery or crank radio and a power bank.
  4. Documents and cash: copies of IDs and records in a waterproof bag, plus some cash in small bills.
  5. Think of everyone: baby supplies, eyeglasses, mobility aids and pet food if you need them.

Make Your Family Emergency Plan

How to build a plan the whole household understands. About 25 seconds, no sound; the steps appear as large on-screen text. Original animation made for SANDIG (see credits).

Read the transcript (text version)
  1. Talk together: include children and older family members.
  2. Pick meeting points: one near home and one outside your neighbourhood.
  3. List contacts: write family, barangay and emergency numbers on paper as a backup.
  4. Learn the routes: walk the way to your evacuation centre and note any barriers.
  5. Practice twice a year: rehearse and update the plan when your household changes.

SANDIG

Sandig means “to rely on” in Filipino. SANDIG helps Filipino families, schools and communities prepare for typhoons, floods, earthquakes, fires and volcanic eruptions. It supports UN Sustainable Development Goal 11: Sustainable Cities and Communities.
